The Argument: Why Now

Old hatred, new tools.

Viral trend
"Training in case she says no": videos simulating aggression against women circulated 2023–2025, some drawing over 175,000 interactions per post.
2023–2025
AI-generated deepfakes — at least 16 documented cases involving 72+ victims. Organized groups now sell fake nude and sex-video generation as a paid service.
Coordinated
Online communities openly share tactics for producing and spreading this content — while platforms remove it only after the harm is already viral.

SaferNet Brasil; Núcleo Jornalismo, 2026

My Thesis

Brazil cannot keep treating digital gender violence as "online drama." It is real violence, accelerated by AI, and it demands the same urgency, funding and enforcement as violence in the street or in the home.

The Argument: The Legal Response

The law is catching up — but not fast enough.

Law 13.718/2018 — Art. 218-C

Criminalizes non-consensual sharing

Sharing intimate images or video without consent: 1 to 5 years in prison, increased by a third to two-thirds when done by an ex-partner or for revenge.

Law 15.123/2025 — Art. 147-B

Targets AI-driven abuse

Adds a 50% sentence increase for psychological violence against women when the offender uses AI or deepfake tools — Brazil's newest direct answer to this crisis.

But Brazil still has no general deepfake law. A broader bill (PL 1.884/2025) remains stuck in Congress, and platforms are not yet legally required to remove this content on their own.

My Proposal for Intervention

Three ways to close the gap between law and enforcement.

1

A legal 24-hour takedown rule

Require platforms to remove reported intimate-image and deepfake content within 24 hours — by law, not as a voluntary courtesy.

2

Digital forensics in every Delegacia da Mulher

Equip and train Brazil's women's police stations to trace and prosecute AI-generated abuse, not just report it.

3

Consent education before the harm starts

Bring digital consent and citizenship into the school curriculum — prevention, not just punishment after the fact.

Closing

The internet doesn't have to be a second scene of the crime.

What Brazil builds into its laws today decides whether it becomes one.
